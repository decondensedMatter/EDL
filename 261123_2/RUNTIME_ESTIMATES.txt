RUN-TIME ESTIMATES — 2026-11-23

SIX-SAMPLE MASTER, UPDATED SINGLE-SHOT READOUT

Active-time limit: 120 minutes across all six specimens. The timer includes
standards hookup/reconnection, reference acquisition, specimen calibration,
fitting, optimization, settling, acquisition, transfer, writing and processing.
Only physical specimen changes are excluded. Unfinished work is logged.

Optimized targets include both signs of every listed magnitude. The magnitude
ceiling includes overshoot. NaClO4 uses 0.1, 0.2, 0.4 and 0.8 V; LLZO adds
1.0 V; LaF3 uses 0.1, 0.2, 0.4, 0.8, 1.6 and 2.0 V. The corresponding maximum
bipolar chirp amplitudes are 1.6 Vpp, 2.0 Vpp and 4.0 Vpp, respectively.

ADAPTIVE ACTIVE-TIME FORECAST

  python estimate_session_runtime.py --adaptive --amp --json

After shared standards the scheduler allocates time in proportion to each
remaining specimen's calibration, CPU and acquisition workload. Voltage is now
the outer loop: calibration and fitting advance with each voltage, followed by
all selected charging tests and sweep views at that level before higher exposure.
Chirp voltage is compared by Vpp/2; charging uses final-voltage magnitude and may
overshoot up to the overall sample limit. The scheduler chooses one dwell scale and initially
reduces intermediate charging durations uniformly, retaining time endpoints.
If a selected level cannot finish within its share, higher levels are not started.
Unused time carries forward to remaining specimens; already saved data survive.

The hold and reset forecasts start at 0.1 and 0.2 s respectively, or at the
higher measured floor. A quadratic increment grows with (voltage/peak limit)^2.
At full scale its peak increment is 0.9 s for hold and 1.8 s for reset. Available
time can scale these increments between 0.1 and 1. The measured floor uses the
next measured voltage at or above the target and retains any larger floor from
a lower voltage. Chirp resets also use the observed global settling time.
Opposite polarities share the more conservative dwell at each magnitude.
Measured floors and replay requirements can make a group infeasible. Actual
optimized peaks, replay extensions and compiled durations are checked separately.

Illustrative all-six forecast: four minutes for shared standards, 12 seconds
per calibration trace (192 specimen traces for the full progressive grid),
90 seconds for the two polarity fits at EACH charging voltage level, two seconds
of CPU per signed target/time, two seconds per capture for setup/transfer/saving,
a 50 ms settling floor and a 20 ms loaded minimum charge time. Progressive fits
are additional work, so the previous 119.92-minute complete-voltage example no
longer applies. The updated estimate is 119.64 active minutes, including partial
calibration/fit work, with 80 of 324 signed jobs and 243 of 297 chirp views:

  Each NaClO4: 17.52 min; 12 signed jobs, 36 chirp views; highest level incomplete.
  LLZO:        21.35 min; 16 signed jobs, 45 chirp views; highest level incomplete.
  LaF3:        24.21 min; 16 signed jobs, 54 chirp views; 1.6 V partial, 2 V not reached.
  Shared standards: 4.00 min.

This is a conservative workload scenario, not a measured completion promise.
The estimator assumes the selected controls are feasible and peak equals target;
actual overshoot and replay extensions can change time and coverage. Faster measured calibration
and fitting may allow more levels. Qualification failures or slow I/O can leave
more unfinished. The shared 7200-second limit is retained in every case.
These estimates use one current path and the updated readout per run. The
amplified path applies a nominal 400 kHz current-bandwidth ceiling. The two
paths have the same example charge durations because the assumed 20 ms
sample-loaded floor dominates both instrument floors. The legacy 256-average
readout has a different acquisition cost (see its section below).

Scans alone exceed the 120-minute limit by 21.9780 minutes even with the optimistic
nominal instrument floor (168.75 ns). The default sample tests use millisecond
edges, so they cannot qualify that nominal lower limit. Independent held-out
sample voltage records determine the loaded minimum: for each record take the
largest of its programmed edge duration, ten scope sample intervals and ten
effective rise times. Multiply the smallest qualifying interval by the number
of optimization intervals (six by default); hardware timing can raise it further.

For an illustrative qualified minimum charge time of 20 ms on all six specimens,
the same 1,593 captures require 148.5308 minutes before standards, calibration,
CPU and extra I/O. Each NaClO4 then takes 22.07 minutes, LLZO 28.32 and
LaF3 31.92. The 20 ms value is a scenario input;
the actual minimum must come from measurements for each installed specimen.
Standards, sample calibration and CPU times have not been measured. The estimate
uses six charge durations, all signed targets, four captures over three matched
variants per target/time, and fast/middle/slow native sweep views for every
shape and Vpp. Each chirp view repeats two complete round trips with one second
per leg and has driven zero before and after the sweep. These records cover
parts of separate repetitions; they cannot establish whole-sweep energy.

Timing assumptions: 2 extra seconds per capture for setup/transfer/writing.
The charge term includes pre-reset, delay to end of view (5% end padding), and a
full protocol guard after acquisition. For charge target V and positive bound B,
hold = 0.1 + 0.9*(V/B)^2 s (zero for immediate discharge);
reset = 0.2 + 1.8*(V/B)^2 s. This scenario assumes realized peak equals target,
nominal hardware timing before qualification of the loaded sample/fixture, all model checks pass, and measured settling adds no
extra dwell. Actual coefficients, voltage excursions, quantization, memory depth,
step response, settling/hold extension and throughput affect the realized run.
An extra second per capture adds 26.55 minutes for 1,593 captures. At 4 s overhead
and a 1 s settling floor, scan acquisition alone requires 240.87 minutes.

  python estimate_session_runtime.py --overhead-s 4 --settling-floor-s 1 --json

CHIRPS — ADD SEPARATELY

At one second per sweep leg, native mode applies 4 seconds per voltage/shape.
Stimulus-only totals: 1.0 minute per NaClO4 specimen, 1.2 minutes for LLZO and
1.4 minutes for LaF3 (15, 18 or 21 records). Those are NOT complete bench times.

The full record at 100 MSa/s contains about 401 million points PER CHANNEL.
Two channels transferred in ASCII can dominate duration. At a purely illustrative
effective throughput of 10 MiB/s and 16 ASCII bytes per channel sample, native
chirps alone would take approximately 307, 369 or 430 minutes, respectively,
including the estimator's 2-second overhead. This is conditional on sufficient
installed scope memory; otherwise that acquisition is unavailable. Throughput has
not been measured here. Do not quote the stimulus-only minutes as total run time.

A 4-second stored ARB at 160 MSa/s needs 640M points. It cannot run in either 1M
standard or 16M optional generator memory. It is listed as infeasible instead of
assigning a misleading finite runtime. Shorter ARB durations can be explicitly
selected, but their low-frequency sampling/settling value must be assessed.

WAVEFORM-ONLY / UNCHANGED READOUT VERSION

The original 256 averages, three-period capture and 100-second timeout remain.
For the default native chirp, even the optimistic trigger-overlap bound exceeds
the timeout. Long optimized protocols likewise cannot complete. These records are
reported before output rather than changing the readout behind this version's
name. A complete default experiment therefore has no valid completion-time
estimate under the unchanged settings. Use the updated readout for such cases.
